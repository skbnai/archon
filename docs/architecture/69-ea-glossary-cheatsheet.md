# ENTERPRISE ARCHITECTURE Glossary & Cheat Sheet

## Glossary (A–Z)

**ABAC** — Attribute-Based Access Control. Authorisation policy where access decisions are based on attributes of the user, resource, and environment rather than predefined roles.

**ADR** — Architecture Decision Record. A short document capturing an architecture decision, its context, the options considered, and the rationale for the choice made.

**AES-256** — Advanced Encryption Standard with 256-bit key. Symmetric block cipher — quantum-resistant. Preferred encryption standard for data at rest.

**AIOps** — Artificial Intelligence for IT Operations. Using ML to automate IT operations tasks: anomaly detection, root cause analysis, and autonomous remediation.

**API Economy** — Business model where APIs are treated as products that enable third-party development, monetisation, and ecosystem creation.

**API Gateway** — Entry point for all API traffic that enforces authentication, authorisation, rate limiting, routing, transformation, and observability.

**ARB** — Architecture Review Board. Governance body that reviews and approves significant architecture decisions and proposals.

**AsyncAPI** — AsyncAPI specification. Open standard for defining event-driven, message-based APIs — the OpenAPI equivalent for asynchronous systems.

**BCBS 239** — Basel Committee Banking Supervision Principle 239. Regulation governing risk data aggregation and risk reporting capabilities for systemically important banks.

**BIAN** — Banking Industry Architecture Network. Framework of standardised banking service domains and service operations.

**Blast Radius** — The maximum scope of damage from a security incident or system failure.

**BCP** — Business Continuity Plan. Plan ensuring critical business functions can continue during and after a disaster.

**Build vs. Buy** — Architecture decision framework: should a capability be built in-house or purchased.

**C2PA** — Coalition for Content Provenance and Authenticity. Standard for cryptographically signing AI-generated content to verify its origin.

**CAIO** — Chief AI Officer. Executive responsible for enterprise AI strategy, governance, and deployment.

**CIAM** — Customer Identity and Access Management. Identity platform managing external customer identities, authentication, and consent.

**Circuit Breaker** — Design pattern that prevents cascading failures by stopping calls to a failing dependency after a failure threshold is exceeded.

**CloudEvents** — CNCF specification standardising the structure of events generated by cloud services.

**CMDB** — Configuration Management Database. Repository of all IT assets and their relationships.

**Conway's Law** — Observation that organisations design systems that mirror their own communication structure.

**CRQC** — Cryptographically Relevant Quantum Computer. A quantum computer powerful enough to break current public-key cryptography (RSA, ECDSA).

**CSRD** — Corporate Sustainability Reporting Directive. EU directive mandating large companies report on environmental, social, and governance factors.

**CVE** — Common Vulnerabilities and Exposures. Standardised list of publicly known software vulnerabilities.

**CycloneDX** — OWASP SBOM standard. Machine-readable format for Software Bill of Materials.

**Data Mesh** — Architectural pattern where data ownership and quality responsibility is distributed to domain teams, with a central data platform providing self-service infrastructure.

**DAST** — Dynamic Application Security Testing. Security testing method that tests a running application from outside, simulating real attacks.

**DR** — Disaster Recovery. Processes and technologies for restoring IT systems and data after a major failure or disaster.

**DORA** — DevOps Research and Assessment. Research programme identifying four key metrics of software delivery performance.

**DORA (EU)** — Digital Operational Resilience Act. Regulation mandating operational resilience for EU financial institutions.

**DP-SGD** — Differentially Private Stochastic Gradient Descent. Training algorithm that adds calibrated noise to prevent training data memorisation.

**DTO** — Digital Twin of the Organisation. A live, computable model of the enterprise — systems, capabilities, costs, risks, dependencies.

**ECDSA** — Elliptic Curve Digital Signature Algorithm. Current standard for digital signatures — quantum-vulnerable.

**EDA** — Event-Driven Architecture. Architecture pattern where services communicate via events rather than direct calls.

**EU AI Act** — EU Artificial Intelligence Act. World's first comprehensive AI regulation. Classifies AI by risk tier (unacceptable, high, limited, minimal).

**Event Mesh** — Distributed event routing infrastructure connecting multiple event brokers across clouds and regions.

**FIDO2** — Fast Identity Online 2. Authentication standard enabling passwordless login using public-key cryptography.

**FinOps** — Cloud Financial Operations. Practice of optimising cloud costs through engineering, finance, and business alignment.

**Fitness Function** — Automated test that measures an architecture characteristic and ensures compliance with design intent.

**GDPR** — General Data Protection Regulation. EU data protection law governing personal data processing.

**GPAI** — General Purpose AI. EU AI Act category for AI models trained at large scale capable of performing many tasks.

**GraphQL** — Query language for APIs that allows clients to request exactly the data they need.

**HITL** — Human-in-the-Loop. Governance pattern where human review or approval is required at specified points in an AI decision process.

**HNDL** — Harvest Now Decrypt Later. Attack strategy where adversaries archive currently encrypted data to decrypt later using a quantum computer.

**HSM** — Hardware Security Module. Dedicated hardware device for secure key generation, storage, and cryptographic operations.

**IaC** — Infrastructure as Code. Managing infrastructure through machine-readable configuration files (Terraform, Pulumi, Bicep).

**IDP (Identity)** — Identity Provider. Service that authenticates users and issues tokens (Entra ID, Okta, PingIdentity).

**IDP (Platform)** — Internal Developer Platform. Self-service portal enabling developers to provision, deploy, and manage services.

**ISO/IEC 27090** — International standard providing guidance on AI cybersecurity including prompt injection and adversarial attacks.

**ISO 20022** — International standard for financial messaging. XML-based successor to SWIFT MT formats.

**JWT** — JSON Web Token. Compact, URL-safe token format for representing claims between parties.

**JIT** — Just-in-Time. Access provisioning model where permissions are granted only when needed for a specific task.

**KMS** — Key Management Service. Cloud service for creating, managing, and controlling cryptographic keys.

**KPIs** — Key Performance Indicators. Measurable values used to track progress toward strategic objectives.

**LLM** — Large Language Model. AI model trained on vast text corpora capable of natural language understanding and generation.

**LLM Gateway** — Centralised entry point for all LLM API calls that enforces authentication, PII scrubbing, injection detection, model routing.

**MACH** — Microservices, API-first, Cloud-native, Headless. Architecture philosophy emphasising composable, independently deployable technology components.

**mTLS** — Mutual TLS. TLS where both client and server present certificates — authenticates both parties.

**ML-DSA** — Module Lattice Digital Signature Algorithm (CRYSTALS-Dilithium). NIST-standardised post-quantum signature algorithm.

**ML-KEM** — Module Lattice Key Encapsulation Mechanism (CRYSTALS-Kyber). NIST-standardised post-quantum key exchange algorithm.

**MTTR** — Mean Time to Recovery. Average time from a failure being detected to service being restored.

**MVA** — Minimum Viable Architecture. The smallest architectural foundation that supports current needs while enabling future evolution.

**NHI** — Non-Human Identity. Identities for machines, services, AI agents, and automated processes.

**NIST PQC** — NIST Post-Quantum Cryptography Standardisation. Programme that selected ML-KEM, ML-DSA, and SLH-DSA as quantum-resistant standards.

**NPV** — Net Present Value. Financial metric calculating the present value of future cash flows minus the initial investment.

**OIDC** — OpenID Connect. Authentication layer on top of OAuth 2.0.

**OPA** — Open Policy Agent. CNCF policy engine for unified policy enforcement across the stack.

**OBO** — On-Behalf-Of. OAuth2 flow where a service exchanges a user's token for a new token to call downstream services.

**PAM** — Privileged Access Management. Security controls for managing, monitoring, and controlling access to privileged accounts.

**PBC** — Packaged Business Capability. Self-contained, independently deployable business capability exposed via APIs.

**PCI-DSS** — Payment Card Industry Data Security Standard. Security standard mandating controls for organisations handling cardholder data.

**PII** — Personally Identifiable Information. Any data that can identify a specific individual.

**PKI** — Public Key Infrastructure. Framework of policies, hardware, software, and procedures for managing digital certificates.

**PQC** — Post-Quantum Cryptography. Cryptographic algorithms designed to be secure against both classical and quantum computers.

**PSD2** — Payment Services Directive 2. EU directive mandating open banking, strong customer authentication, and third-party access.

**RAG** — Retrieval-Augmented Generation. AI pattern combining LLM generation with retrieval from a knowledge base.

**RBAC** — Role-Based Access Control. Access control model where permissions are assigned to roles.

**RFC 8693** — OAuth 2.0 Token Exchange. Standard protocol for exchanging one OAuth token for another.

**RTO** — Recovery Time Objective. Maximum acceptable time a system can be unavailable after a failure.

**RPO** — Recovery Point Objective. Maximum acceptable amount of data loss measured in time.

**SAML** — Security Assertion Markup Language. XML-based standard for exchanging authentication and authorisation data.

**SAST** — Static Application Security Testing. Analysing source code for security vulnerabilities without executing the program.

**SBB** — Solution Building Block. TOGAF term for a specific product, tool, or technology used to implement an Architecture Building Block.

**SBOM** — Software Bill of Materials. Machine-readable inventory of all software components and dependencies in an application.

**SCA** — Software Composition Analysis. Automated scanning of open-source dependencies for known vulnerabilities and licence compliance.

**SHAP** — SHapley Additive exPlanations. Model interpretability technique that assigns each feature an importance value for a particular prediction.

**SLI** — Service Level Indicator. Specific metric used to measure service performance (e.g., request latency P99).

**SLO** — Service Level Objective. Target value for an SLI over a time window.

**SRE** — Site Reliability Engineering. Discipline applying software engineering to IT operations.

**SR 11-7** — US Federal Reserve Supervisory Guidance 11-7. Defines model risk management requirements for US banking organisations.

**SVID** — SPIFFE Verifiable Identity Document. Cryptographic identity document issued by SPIRE to workloads.

**SWIFT** — Society for Worldwide Interbank Financial Telecommunication. Messaging network for international financial transactions.

**SPIFFE** — Secure Production Identity Framework For Everyone. Open standard for workload identity in distributed systems.

**SPIRE** — SPIFFE Runtime Environment. Reference implementation of the SPIFFE standard.

**TLPT** — Threat-Led Penetration Testing. DORA-mandated red team exercise where external specialists simulate real-world cyber attacks.

**TOGAF** — The Open Group Architecture Framework. Enterprise architecture methodology covering the Architecture Development Method (ADM).

**TVP** — Thinnest Viable Platform. Platform engineering principle: only centralise capabilities that are genuinely better centralised.

**Team Topologies** — Organisational design framework defining four team types: Stream-aligned, Platform, Enabling, Complicated-subsystem.

**vLLM** — Open-source LLM inference engine optimised for high throughput and low latency using PagedAttention.

**VMSS** — Virtual Machine Scale Set (Azure) / Auto Scaling Group (AWS). Cloud infrastructure that automatically scales capacity based on demand.

**WAF** — Web Application Firewall. Security control filtering HTTP/HTTPS traffic to protect against common attacks.

**WORM** — Write Once Read Many. Storage that prevents modification or deletion of data after writing.

**W3C Trace Context** — W3C standard for distributed tracing — defines HTTP headers for passing trace context across service boundaries.

**Zero Trust** — Security model: 'never trust, always verify.' No implicit trust based on network location.

**Zachman Framework** — Enterprise architecture framework organising architectural artefacts across stakeholder perspectives and abstraction levels.

**ZTA** — Zero Trust Architecture. Implementation of Zero Trust principles through identity, device, network, and data controls.

## Regulation Quick Reference

**DORA (EU).** Scope: Financial institutions in EU/EEA. Key architecture obligations: ICT risk management, third-party risk register, resilience testing (TLPT every 3yr), incident reporting (4-hr initial), concentration risk. Penalties: Supervisory measures, capital add-ons.

**EU AI Act.** Scope: All AI systems used or placed on market in EU. Key obligations: High-risk AI conformity assessment, Annex IV technical docs, human oversight, GPAI transparency, post-market monitoring. Penalties: €30M or 6% global turnover (higher).

**SR 11-7.** Scope: US banking organisations (Fed-regulated). Key obligations: Model inventory, independent validation, ongoing monitoring, board governance, conceptual soundness documentation. Penalties: Regulatory findings, formal agreements, capital implications.

**GDPR.** Scope: All processors of EU personal data. Key obligations: Article 22 no solely automated decisions, Article 17 right to erasure, Article 25 privacy by design. Penalties: €20M or 4% global turnover (higher).

**PCI-DSS v4.0.** Scope: Card payment processors and merchants globally. Key obligations: Network segmentation, encryption in transit and at rest, access control, audit logging, penetration testing annually. Penalties: Fines, card processing suspension.

**BCBS 239.** Scope: Global systemically important banks. Key obligations: Risk data aggregation, accuracy, completeness, timeliness, adaptability of risk reporting. Penalties: Pillar 2 capital requirements, supervisory measures.

**NIS2.** Scope: Essential and important entities including financial in EU/EEA. Key obligations: Cybersecurity risk management, incident reporting, supply chain security, encryption. Penalties: €10M or 2% global turnover.

**CSRD.** Scope: Large EU companies phased from 2025. Key obligations: Technology carbon reporting (Scope 1/2/3), sustainability due diligence, double materiality assessment. Penalties: Public reporting obligation, auditor sign-off.

## Post-Quantum Cryptography Migration Guide

**HNDL Risk:** Adversaries are archiving encrypted data TODAY to decrypt when CRQCs arrive (est. 2028-2031). Long-lived sensitive data (10+ yr retention) must be protected now. NIST mandate: migrate to PQC standards by 2035; financial services regulators expect earlier.

**ML-KEM (Kyber).** Replaces: ECDH, RSA-KE. NIST Status: FIPS 203 — Final. Use case: TLS key exchange, encrypted messaging.

**ML-DSA (Dilithium).** Replaces: ECDSA, RSA. NIST Status: FIPS 204 — Final. Use case: JWT signing, certificate signing, code signing.

**SLH-DSA (SPHINCS+).** Replaces: ECDSA, RSA. NIST Status: FIPS 205 — Final. Use case: Backup signature scheme (hash-based, no lattice).

**AES-256.** Replaces: AES-128. NIST Status: Already quantum-safe. Use case: Data at rest, data in transit (symmetric).

**SHA-3.** Replaces: SHA-256. NIST Status: Already quantum-safe. Use case: Hashing, HMAC, integrity checking.

**RSA-2048, ECDSA P-256, X25519.** Status: QUANTUM-VULNERABLE — migrate. Use cases: Legacy TLS, certificate signing, JWT, SSH, code signing.

**Migration Sequence:** 1st: TLS certificates for internet-facing services (Hybrid ML-KEM + X25519). 2nd: JWT signing keys (ML-DSA). 3rd: PKI root and intermediate CAs. 4th: SWIFT/interbank messaging (coordinate with counterparties). 5th: HSMs and key material. 6th: SSH keys and code signing. 7th: Legacy data at rest (archival).

## Distinguished Architect Interview Signals

**Technical Depth.** Principal: Technical depth deep in 2-3 areas, surface-level elsewhere. Distinguished: Deep across multiple domains, explicitly acknowledges knowledge boundaries.

**Business Connection.** Principal: Can explain business impact when asked. Distinguished: Leads with business impact, translates every technical decision to financial/strategic terms.

**Ambiguity Handling.** Principal: Asks clarifying questions before answering. Distinguished: Makes assumptions explicit, answers with them, then asks if the assumption holds.

**Tradeoff Articulation.** Principal: Describes tradeoffs when discussing options. Distinguished: Articulates tradeoffs before being asked, explains which tradeoff they would make and why.

**Regulatory Awareness.** Principal: Aware of relevant regulations, mentions them. Distinguished: Integrates regulatory requirements into architecture as first-class constraints, knows specific articles/sections.

**Organisational Awareness.** Principal: Technical solution is technically sound. Distinguished: Anticipates organisational and political impediments, designs for adoption, not just correctness.

**Whiteboard Discipline.** Principal: Draws accurate technical diagrams. Distinguished: Draws diagrams that tell a story, labels for the audience in the room.

**Follow-up Challenges.** Principal: Defends original answer, may concede under pressure. Distinguished: Updates view when presented with new information, holds position when pushback is not evidence-based.

**Executive Communication.** Principal: Can summarise in 5 minutes. Distinguished: Has a 30-second version ready, leads with the conclusion, follows with evidence only if asked.
