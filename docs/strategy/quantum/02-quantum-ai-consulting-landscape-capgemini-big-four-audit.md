# Quantum AI Consulting: Capgemini & the Big Four Audit Firms

Capgemini bridges quantum strategy and systems integration. The Big Four (PwC, EY, KPMG) enter via compliance and risk rather than compute acceleration.

## Capgemini: European SI Bridge

**IBM Quantum Hub member (170+ network members) · Q-Labs (UK/Portugal/India) · €22B revenue (~$22B USD)[^1]**

Capgemini fills the European systems integrator gap between quantum strategy (McKinsey, BCG) and quantum hardware (IBM, IonQ). With 350,000+ employees[^1], they execute quantum projects at scale neither boutique firms nor hardware vendors can match.

Their IBM Quantum Hub membership provides structured hardware access without IBM Consulting's exclusivity. They recommend multiple vendors while using IBM as primary anchor.

**Q-Lab Co-Innovation:** Three dedicated quantum R&D centers (UK, Portugal, India) where clients co-develop use cases before production commitment.

**Sector Focus (2025–2026):** Life Sciences (drug discovery), Financial Services (portfolio optimization), Automotive (manufacturing simulation), Aerospace & Defence (trajectory optimization), Energy & Utilities (grid optimization).

**Best Practices:** Start via Q-Lab co-innovation to reduce financial risk. For IBM-heavy accounts (SAP on IBM Cloud), Capgemini's integration creates natural leverage. Engage for quantum sensing and communications, not just computing.

**Anti-Patterns:** Conflating Capgemini's IBM Quantum Hub with IBM Consulting's exclusive access. Treating Q-Lab as production deployment service (it's R&D; production requires separate programme). Expecting Capgemini to compete with MBB on quantum strategy depth.

## Big Four Audit Firms: PwC, EY, KPMG

All three enter quantum through compliance and risk, lacking direct hardware partnerships. Quantum is "another risk to manage" rather than "a capability to deploy."

| Firm | Entry Point | Key Differentiator | Focus |
|---|---|---|---|
| PwC | "Q-Day" risk framing | Board-level quantum risk | Global audit clients; FSI |
| EY | Financial services regulatory quantum readiness | Deep FSI regulatory depth | Banks, insurance, asset management |
| KPMG | "Quantum Dawn" research | Academic credibility; HPC research | Government, higher education, defence |

### PwC Quantum Practice

**Key Offerings:**
- Quantum Risk Assessment: inventory quantum-vulnerable cryptographic systems
- Board-Level Quantum Risk Advisory: quantum risk briefing for audit committees
- PQC Implementation Planning: NIST FIPS adoption, crypto-agility architecture design
- Quantum Computing Benefits Assessment: near-term quantum application use cases

Engagements typically begin through existing audit/risk relationships where PQC compliance surfaces.

### EY Quantum Practice

**"5 C-Suite Steps" Framework:**
1. Assess quantum risk exposure
2. Benchmark competitor quantum investments
3. Identify near-term quantum use cases
4. Build quantum talent pipeline
5. Establish quantum governance board

EY's financial-services focus (most top-20 global banks are EY audit clients) means quantum engagements trigger alongside existing regulatory work — lower sales threshold than standalone advisory.

### KPMG Quantum Practice

**"Quantum Dawn" Research:** Multi-year study of quantum's impact on financial markets and critical infrastructure. Used by central banks and financial regulators globally.

KPMG's direct relationships with national regulators and central banks (via audit relationships) provide access to quantum policy discussions that other audit firms lack.

## Pattern: Compliance-First Entry Points Are Fast Sales Cycles

PwC, EY, KPMG generate revenue immediately because PQC compliance mandates create regulatory demand independent of quantum hardware maturity. EO-14412, FIPS 203–205, DORA, EU Cyber Resilience Act are regulatory facts. The compliance-first engagement is fastest path from "no quantum budget" to "approved quantum project."
